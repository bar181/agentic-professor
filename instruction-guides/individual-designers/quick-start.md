# Quick Start Guide

**Create Your First AMCD Module in 60 Minutes**

---

## What You'll Create

In the next hour, you'll have a complete module outline ready to develop into content. Not finished content—but a solid structure that tells you exactly what to create.

---

## Before You Start

You need:
- One specific skill or concept to teach
- 60 minutes of focused time
- Something to write with (doc, notes app, paper)

**Choose a scope that can be taught in one module (40-75 minutes of learner time).** If your topic is bigger, pick one piece of it.

✅ Good scope: "How to write compelling email subject lines"
❌ Too big: "Email marketing"

✅ Good scope: "Setting up your first Facebook ad campaign"
❌ Too big: "Digital marketing"

---

## Step 1: Define the Outcome (10 minutes)

Answer these questions:

### What will learners be able to DO after this module?

Not "understand" or "know about"—actually DO.

```
After this module, learners will be able to:
_____________________________________________
```

**Test:** Can you watch them do it? Is it observable?

❌ "Understand the importance of email marketing"
✅ "Write 5 email subject lines that follow the curiosity formula"

### Who is this for?

```
This module is for people who:
- Currently: ______________________________
- Want to: ________________________________
- Struggle with: __________________________
```

### What's the one key insight?

If they forget everything else, what's the ONE thing they should remember?

```
The key insight: ___________________________
```

## Step 2: Captivate Outline (10 minutes)

You're creating the hook—the first lesson that makes them care.

### Choose a hook type:

**Frustration Hook:**
"Picture this: [relatable frustrating scenario that your teaching solves]"

**Contrast Hook:**
"Two [people] faced the same [challenge]. One [succeeded], one [failed]. The difference wasn't [obvious thing]—it was [your key insight]."

**Question Hook:**
"What if I told you that [surprising claim about their problem/solution]?"

**Story Hook:**
"Let me tell you about [specific person who transformed using this]..."

### Fill in this outline:

```
CAPTIVATE OUTLINE (Lesson 1)

Hook: [Your chosen hook type - 2-3 sentences]
______________________________________________________
______________________________________________________

Stakes: Why does this matter? What's at risk?
______________________________________________________
______________________________________________________

Big Picture: How does this connect to what they want?
______________________________________________________

Key Insight Preview: The one thing that changes everything
______________________________________________________

Module Preview: "By the end of this module, you'll..."
______________________________________________________
```

## Next: Turn Outline Into Content

This outline is your blueprint. To create the actual content:

### For video courses:
- Use each section as a script outline
- Record Captivate and Orient as talking head or slides
- Realize can be narrated over relevant visuals
- Execute can be screen share or demonstration

### For text-based courses:
- Expand each section into written lessons
- Captivate: 400-600 words
- Orient: 600-800 words
- Realize: 500-700 words
- Execute: 300-500 words + task instructions

### For live workshops:
- Captivate: Opening presentation (5-10 min)
- Orient: Teaching segment (15-20 min)
- Realize: Case study discussion (10-15 min)
- Execute: Hands-on activity (20-30 min)

## Example: Complete Quick Start Output

**Module: Writing Email Subject Lines That Get Opened**

**Outcome:** After this module, learners will be able to write 5 email subject lines using the curiosity-gap formula that consistently outperform generic subject lines.

**Captivate Outline:**
- Hook (Frustration): "You spent 3 hours on that email. Perfect copy. Valuable offer. 4% open rate. No one saw it."
- Stakes: Email success starts before the email. Subject line determines whether anyone reads your work.
- Big Picture: Top email marketers write 10+ subject line variants before choosing one. It's a skill, not luck.
- Key Insight: The curiosity gap—creating just enough intrigue to demand the click.
- Preview: "You'll create 5 subject lines for your next email using the proven curiosity formula."

**Orient Outline:**
- Concept 1: The Curiosity Gap
  - Definition: Creating incomplete information that compels completion
  - Example basic: "Why I stopped using [tool]" vs "Tool review"
  - Example typical: "The mistake I made with [thing] (and how I fixed it)"

- Concept 2: The Three Elements
  - Specificity (numbers, names, details)
  - Relevance (speaks to their situation)
  - Incompleteness (demands the click to resolve)

- Pattern: Good subject line = Specific + Relevant + Incomplete

**Realize Outline:**
- Character: Jamie, solopreneur launching course
- Opening: First launch email. Subject: "New course available!" 3% open rate.
- Challenge: Great course, no opens. Felt like shouting into void.
- Struggle: Tried longer subjects, shorter subjects, emojis. Nothing worked consistently.
- Insight: Discovered curiosity gap when A/B tested "My $47k launch mistake"—18% opens.
- Outcome: Rewrote launch sequence. Average opens went from 4% to 14%.
- Reflection: Now writes 10 subject lines first, picks the most curiosity-creating one.

**Execute Outline:**
- Task: Write 5 subject lines for your next email using the curiosity formula
- Success: 5 subject lines written, each using specific + relevant + incomplete
- Steps:
  1. Choose your next email topic
  2. Write one straightforward subject (the baseline)
  3. Rewrite using specificity
  4. Rewrite using curiosity gap
  5. Create 2 more variations
  6. Choose your top 2 for A/B testing
- Common mistakes: Too clever (confusing), not specific (generic), giving away the answer (no curiosity)
- Verification: 5 lines written, each has specific detail, each creates incomplete curiosity
