# Common Mistakes

**What Other Course Creators Get Wrong (And How to Avoid It)**

---

## Mistake 1: Starting with Information

### The Problem
Most creators jump straight into content: "In this module, we'll learn about X..."

This is the course equivalent of walking up to someone and saying "Let me tell you about my product features."

### Why It Fails
- No motivation established
- Learners don't know why they should care
- High early dropout
- Information without context doesn't stick

### The AMCD Fix
**Always start with Captivate.** Before you teach anything, make them want to learn it.

❌ "In this module, we'll cover email marketing fundamentals..."
✅ "Sarah had 50,000 followers and couldn't pay her rent. Then she discovered she was building the wrong asset..."

**Rule:** If your first lesson could be titled "Introduction to [Topic]," you're making this mistake.

---

## Mistake 2: Explaining Without Examples

### The Problem
Definitions and explanations without concrete examples. Concepts that stay abstract.

"SEO is the practice of optimizing content for search engines. It involves on-page and off-page factors..."

### Why It Fails
- Abstract concepts don't stick
- Learners can't visualize application
- Sounds like a Wikipedia article
- No practical reference point

### The AMCD Fix
**Every concept gets an example.** In Orient lessons, the pattern is: define → show.

❌ "Keywords are search terms that users type into search engines."
✅ "Keywords are what people type when they're looking for you. When someone types 'best protein powder for beginners,' that's a keyword. When they type 'protein powder,' that's a different keyword—shorter, more competitive, less specific to what they actually want."

**Rule:** If you explain something without immediately showing it, add an example.

## Mistake 3: Stories Without Point

### The Problem
Including case studies or stories that don't clearly connect to the concepts being taught.

"Let me tell you about my client John... [long story] ...and that's how he succeeded."

### Why It Fails
- Feels like filler
- Connection to lesson is unclear
- Learners wonder what to take away
- Entertainment without education

### The AMCD Fix
**Stories must illustrate concepts.** In Realize lessons, the story shows Lesson 2 concepts in action.

❌ "John started his business and faced many challenges. He tried different things and eventually succeeded."
✅ "John's first campaign flopped because he targeted keywords that were too broad—remember, high-volume isn't the same as high-intent. When he switched to long-tail keywords, his conversion rate tripled. Same budget, different targeting."

**Rule:** After every story, you should be able to say "This shows [specific concept from Lesson 2]."

---

## Mistake 4: Vague Practice Activities

### The Problem
Execute lessons that say "Now try it yourself!" or "Think about how you could apply this."

### Why It Fails
- Learners don't know what to do
- No way to verify success
- Feels like homework, not practice
- Often skipped entirely

### The AMCD Fix
**Specific task, clear criteria, observable outcome.**

❌ "Now try implementing what you've learned with your own email list."
✅ "Your task: Write 3 email subject lines for your next newsletter using the curiosity-gap formula. Success criteria: Each line must (1) be under 50 characters, (2) create incomplete information, and (3) use at least one specific number or detail."

**Rule:** Can someone else verify completion without your input? If not, your task is too vague.

---

## Mistake 5: Teaching Everything You Know

### The Problem
Including every tip, technique, variation, and edge case you've ever learned about the topic.

### Why It Fails
- Overwhelms learners
- No clear priority
- Hard to implement
- Expert knowledge ≠ beginner needs

### The AMCD Fix
**Teach what they need to DO the thing, not everything you know about the thing.**

❌ A module on email marketing that covers: history of email, 14 different subject line formulas, segmentation strategies, automation workflows, deliverability optimization, A/B testing methodologies...
✅ A module on email subject lines that covers: why subject lines matter, the curiosity-gap formula, how to apply it, practice writing 5 subject lines.

**Rule:** If a beginner couldn't apply it by the end of the module, you've included too much.

---

## Mistake 6: Inconsistent Energy

### The Problem
Teaching with the same energy throughout. Lecture tone for everything, or enthusiasm that exhausts.

### Why It Fails
- Monotonous experience
- Wrong tone for the moment
- Exhausting or boring
- Doesn't match what learners need

### The AMCD Fix
**Match energy to lesson purpose.**

| Lesson | Energy | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Captivate | 7/10 - Enthusiastic | Building excitement |
| Orient | 5/10 - Calm | Reducing cognitive load |
| Realize | 6/10 - Warm | Creating emotional connection |
| Execute | 4/10 - Direct | Clarity for action |

**Rule:** Record yourself. Does your energy match the lesson type?

## Mistake 7: No Character in Stories

### The Problem
Case studies that are generic or about "someone" rather than a specific person with a name and context.

### Why It Fails
- Hard to follow
- No emotional investment
- Abstract rather than concrete
- Forgettable

### The AMCD Fix
**Give your character a name, situation, and stakes.**

❌ "Consider a marketer who wants to improve their email open rates..."
✅ "Marcus runs a consulting business. He sends a weekly newsletter to 2,400 subscribers. His open rate was stuck at 12% for six months. That meant only 288 people were seeing the content he spent 3 hours creating."

**Rule:** If you can't visualize the person, your learners can't either.

---

## Mistake 8: Skipping the Struggle

### The Problem
Case studies where everything works perfectly. No mistakes, no challenges, just success.

### Why It Fails
- Unrelatable (real life has struggles)
- Doesn't prepare for obstacles
- Feels like marketing, not teaching
- Learners don't learn from clean paths

### The AMCD Fix
**Show the struggle before the solution.**

❌ "Sarah implemented email marketing and saw great results."
✅ "Sarah's first three email campaigns bombed. 4% open rate. 0.3% click rate. She almost gave up. Then she noticed something in her best-performing email—the subject line had asked a question instead of making a claim."

**Rule:** What went wrong before it went right?

---

## Mistake 9: No Success Criteria

### The Problem
Practice activities without a clear definition of "done" or "good."

### Why It Fails
- Learners don't know if they succeeded
- Creates anxiety
- Completion feels arbitrary
- Learning can't be verified

### The AMCD Fix
**Tell them exactly what success looks like.**

❌ "Create some email subject lines."
✅ "Create 5 subject lines. Checklist: □ Under 50 characters □ Uses curiosity gap □ Includes one specific detail □ You'd open an email with this subject"

**Rule:** Could someone check their own work against your criteria?

---

## Mistake 10: Too Long Modules

### The Problem
Modules that try to cover too much and end up being 3+ hours of content.

### Why It Fails
- Learners can't complete in one sitting
- Progress feels slow
- Easy to get lost
- Hard to know where to resume

### The AMCD Fix
**One module = one teachable concept = 40-75 minutes.**

❌ "Email Marketing Masterclass" (one 4-hour module)
✅ Split into: "Email List Building" + "Subject Line Mastery" + "Email Copywriting" + "Email Automation" (four 45-60 min modules)

**Rule:** If it can't be done in one focused session, break it up.

## Self-Assessment Checklist

Before you publish a module, check:

### Captivate
- [ ] Starts with hook, not information?
- [ ] Establishes why they should care?
- [ ] Previews the outcome?

### Orient
- [ ] Every concept has an example?
- [ ] Only essential concepts included?
- [ ] Progresses from simple to complex?

### Realize
- [ ] Has a named character?
- [ ] Shows the struggle, not just success?
- [ ] Clearly connects to Lesson 2 concepts?

### Execute
- [ ] Task is specific?
- [ ] Success criteria are observable?
- [ ] Common mistakes are warned against?

**If you checked everything:** You avoided the common mistakes.

**If you missed any:** Revise before publishing.

---

## The Mindset Shift

Most of these mistakes come from one place: **teaching like an expert** instead of **teaching for the learner**.

| Expert Mindset | Learner-Centered Mindset |
|---------------|------------------------|
| What do I know? | What do they need? |
| Comprehensive coverage | Focused application |
| Logical organization | Motivational organization |
| Assume interest | Create interest |
| Content-first | Outcome-first |

AMCD forces the learner-centered approach because the structure requires it.
