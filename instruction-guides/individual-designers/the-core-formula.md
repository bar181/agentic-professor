# The CORE Formula

**The 4-Lesson Module Structure That Works Every Time**

---

## The Formula

Every module in your course should have exactly 4 lessons:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     THE CORE FORMULA                         │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                              │
│  C - CAPTIVATE  →  O - ORIENT  →  R - REALIZE  →  E - EXECUTE │
│                                                              │
│  "Why care?"      "How work?"     "When apply?"   "Do it!"  │
│                                                              │
│  Hook them        Teach them      Show them       Let them  │
│                                                              │
│  5-10 min         10-20 min       10-15 min       15-30 min │
│                                                              │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

That's it. Four lessons. Each with a specific job. Repeatable for every module in your course.

---

## C - Captivate (Lesson 1)

### The Job
Create engagement before education. Make learners care about what they're about to learn.

### What Goes Here
- A hook that creates curiosity
- Real stakes (why this matters to them)
- A preview of what's possible
- The "so what" before the "here's how"

### What Doesn't Go Here
- Implementation details
- Technical explanations
- Step-by-step instructions
- Everything you know about the topic

### Duration
5-10 minutes. This is an appetizer, not the meal.

### The Energy
High. Enthusiastic. Story-driven. This is where you sell the lesson.

### Template

```
HOOK: Start with a story, question, or surprising fact
      "Picture this..." / "What if I told you..." / "[Person] had [problem]..."

BIG PICTURE: Why does this matter in the real world?
      Connect to something they already care about

KEY INSIGHT: What's the one thing that changes everything?
      The insight they'll remember even if they forget details

PREVIEW: What will they be able to do after this module?
      Concrete capability, not abstract knowledge
```

### Example: Productivity Module

❌ **Bad Captivate:**
"In this module, we'll learn about the Pomodoro Technique, which was invented in the late 1980s by Francesco Cirillo. The technique uses a timer to break work into intervals..."

✅ **Good Captivate:**
"Last Tuesday, I watched my client Sara burn out. She'd been 'busy' for 16 hours and accomplished almost nothing meaningful. Sound familiar? Here's the thing: productivity isn't about time management. It's about energy management. And there's a simple technique that top performers use to get 4 hours of deep work done while others spin their wheels all day. By the end of this module, you'll be using it too."

## O - Orient (Lesson 2)

### The Job
Build systematic understanding. Explain how things work—clearly, with examples.

### What Goes Here
- Core concepts defined simply
- Examples that progress from basic to complex
- Common misconceptions addressed
- The mental model they need

### What Doesn't Go Here
- Assumptions about prior knowledge
- Undefined jargon
- Implementation without explanation
- Everything at once

### Duration
10-20 minutes. This is where the teaching happens.

### The Energy
Calm. Methodical. Patient. Like a good tutor.

### Template

```
CONNECT: Bridge from Lesson 1's hook
      "Remember [hook scenario]? Here's why that happened..."

CONCEPT 1: Define it simply, then show it
      - One sentence definition
      - Why it matters
      - Simple example
      - Slightly harder example

CONCEPT 2: Build on Concept 1
      - Definition
      - Connection to Concept 1
      - Examples showing the relationship

CONCEPT 3: (if needed)
      - Follow same pattern

PATTERNS: What should they remember?
      "The key patterns are: 1) ... 2) ... 3) ..."
```

### Example: Productivity Module

❌ **Bad Orient:**
"Time blocking is when you block time. Here are 47 different ways people time block. Let me read you the Wikipedia article about it..."

✅ **Good Orient:**
"Let's break down why some people accomplish more in 4 hours than others do in 10.

**Concept 1: Focus Blocks**
A focus block is a protected period where you do ONE thing. No email. No Slack. Just the work.

Here's a simple example: You block 9-11am for writing. During those two hours, you write. Period.

Here's what typically happens without it: You intend to write, but check email first. Then Slack. Then a quick meeting. By the time you start writing, it's 11:30 and you have another meeting at noon.

**Concept 2: Energy Matching**
Your energy varies throughout the day. Focus blocks work when you match them to your energy.

If you're sharpest in the morning, that's when your hardest work happens.
If you're useless before coffee, don't schedule deep work at 7am.

**The Pattern:**
1. Identify your 2-3 highest energy hours
2. Protect them with focus blocks
3. Put your most important work there
4. Everything else goes elsewhere"

## R - Realize (Lesson 3)

### The Job
Show the concepts in action through story. Bridge from understanding to application.

### What Goes Here
- A character facing the challenge
- Decisions and consequences
- The concepts from Lesson 2 in context
- What can go right and wrong

### What Doesn't Go Here
- More theory
- Abstract explanations
- Passive descriptions
- Perfect outcomes only

### Duration
10-15 minutes. Long enough to tell a story, short enough to maintain tension.

### The Energy
Warm. Narrative. Empathetic. Like sharing a story with a friend.

### Template

```
DROP IN: Start in the middle of the action
      "[Character] stared at [specific situation]. This wasn't working..."

CONTEXT: What are they trying to accomplish?
      Why does it matter to them?

THE STRUGGLE: What's hard about this?
      Show the real challenge, not the theoretical one

THE INSIGHT: When do they figure it out?
      Connect to Lesson 2 concepts

THE OUTCOME: What happens?
      Show results—including imperfect ones

THE LESSON: What would they do differently?
      Reflection that helps learners
```

### Example: Productivity Module

❌ **Bad Realize:**
"Here is an example of time blocking. Consider a knowledge worker who implements time blocking. They would block their time and then work during those blocks..."

✅ **Good Realize:**
"Marcus stared at his calendar. Three meetings, none critical. Slack pinging constantly. And his product launch presentation—due tomorrow—was still a skeleton.

He'd been 'working' on it for a week. Somehow, it never got done.

That night, Marcus tried something different. He looked at his energy patterns. Mornings were his peak time. But every morning started with email and meetings.

The next day, he blocked 7-9am. Calendar said 'FOCUS: Presentation.' He turned off Slack. Put his phone in another room.

At 9:03am, he had the first complete draft he'd produced all week.

'I did more in two hours than the previous five days,' he told me later. 'I thought I needed more time. I needed less time, better protected.'

What made it work:
- Energy matching: Morning peak → hardest work
- Protection: No notifications, no interruptions
- Single focus: Presentation only, nothing else

What he'd do differently next time: Block the night before, so the meeting invites can't steal the slot."

## E - Execute (Lesson 4)

### The Job
Let learners do it themselves. Guided practice with clear outcomes.

### What Goes Here
- A specific task to complete
- Clear success criteria
- Common mistakes to avoid
- Resources they need

### What Doesn't Go Here
- More lecturing
- Theory review
- Vague suggestions
- "Think about how you might..."

### Duration
15-30 minutes of actual work (instruction can be brief)

### The Energy
Direct. Practical. Coach-like. No fluff.

### Template

```
YOUR TASK: State exactly what they'll create
      "Build/Create/Design your [specific deliverable]"

BEFORE YOU START: What do they need?
      Checklist of prerequisites

SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE: How will they know they're done?
      Observable criteria

STEP-BY-STEP: Walk them through
      1. Do this
         [What success looks like]
      2. Then this
         [What success looks like]
      3. Then this
         [What success looks like]

COMMON MISTAKES: What goes wrong?
      - If you see X, do Y
      - Don't do Z

VERIFY: Final checklist
      □ Did you do A?
      □ Did you do B?
      □ Does it work?
```

### Example: Productivity Module

❌ **Bad Execute:**
"Now think about how you could apply time blocking in your own life. Consider your schedule and energy levels. Perhaps try blocking some time tomorrow."

✅ **Good Execute:**
"**Your Task:** Create your first weekly focus block schedule

**Before You Start:**
- [ ] Have your calendar open
- [ ] Know your meeting commitments for next week
- [ ] Download the Focus Block template (link)

**Success Criteria:**
- 3 focus blocks scheduled for next week
- Each block is 2 hours minimum
- Blocks are in your peak energy window
- Calendar shows them as 'busy' to others

**Steps:**

1. **Identify your peak energy window**
   Look at the past week. When did you do your best thinking?
   Most people: morning. Some: late night. Know yours.
   ✓ Write it down: My peak is ____ to ____.

2. **Find 3 two-hour windows next week**
   Look at your calendar. Find gaps in your peak window.
   ✓ Mark them tentatively.

3. **Create the blocks**
   Add calendar events titled 'FOCUS: [Your key project]'
   Set them as 'busy' so others can't book over them.
   ✓ Three blocks created.

4. **Set up your environment**
   For each block, decide:
   - Where will you work?
   - What will you turn off?
   - What's the ONE thing for each block?
   ✓ Write it in the calendar event.

**Common Mistakes:**
- Blocking 'productive time' without a specific task = wasted
- Checking 'just one' email first = death of focus
- Starting with 4-hour blocks = unsustainable

**Verify:**
□ 3 blocks scheduled
□ Each has a specific project/task assigned
□ Calendar shows 'busy' to prevent overwrite
□ You know what to turn off during each block

**Done.** You now have protected focus time for next week."

## Putting It Together

A complete module looks like this:

| Lesson | Purpose | Duration | Vibe |
|--------|---------|----------|------|
| Captivate | Why care? | 5-10 min | Energetic, story |
| Orient | How work? | 10-20 min | Calm, systematic |
| Realize | When apply? | 10-15 min | Warm, narrative |
| Execute | Do it! | 15-30 min | Direct, practical |

**Total module time: 40-75 minutes**

This structure works for:
- Technical skills (coding, design, data)
- Creative skills (writing, video, art)
- Business skills (marketing, sales, leadership)
- Personal development (productivity, habits, mindset)

---

## Why Four Lessons?

### Too Few = Gaps
- 2 lessons: Where's the practice?
- 3 lessons: Where's the motivation OR the application?

### Too Many = Fragmentation
- 5+ lessons: Learners lose the thread
- Long courses: Completion collapses

### Four = Complete Cycle
- Motivation → Understanding → Application → Practice
- Each feeds the next
- Nothing critical is missing

---

## The Quality Difference

This structure is what separates professional courses from amateur ones.

| Amateur Course | CORE-Structured Course |
|----------------|----------------------|
| Starts with theory | Starts with why it matters |
| Information dump | Systematic build |
| Examples are afterthoughts | Stories are central |
| Practice is optional | Practice is required |
| Variable quality | Consistent quality |
